# Jainism — Agamas and related texts

Links only. No scripture text is copied here. Jain canonical literature is often grouped as the Agamas (and related texts), in Prakrit and Sanskrit, with later vernacular commentarial traditions. Each link names its source, publisher, and licence or terms as the site states them.

## Original languages (Prakrit and Sanskrit)

- [Sacred Texts, Jainism](https://sacred-texts.com/jai/index.htm): a free collection of Jain scriptures and related texts in transcription and translation. Publisher: Internet Sacred Text Archive. Licence: the site states that most of its texts are freely available to read without an account; many texts are in the public domain, while the site's design and some commentary remain protected ([terms of service](https://sacred-texts.com/terms); [copyrights and public domain](https://sacred-texts.com/copyrights)).
- [GRETIL](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil.html): scholarly electronic Jain texts among its Indian-language holdings. Publisher: GRETIL, University of Göttingen. Licence: terms not stated on the introduction page ([GRETIL home](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil.html)).

## English

- [Sacred Texts, Jainism](https://sacred-texts.com/jai/index.htm): public-domain English translations of Jain texts as hosted by the Archive. Publisher: Internet Sacred Text Archive. Licence: the site states that most of its texts are freely available to read without an account; many texts are in the public domain, while the site's design and some commentary remain protected ([terms of service](https://sacred-texts.com/terms); [copyrights and public domain](https://sacred-texts.com/copyrights)).

## Other languages

No complete, clearly licensed modern-language set of the Agamas on a single free hub was confirmed, beyond what appears in the collections above. GRETIL also lists some later Indian-language materials (licence: terms not stated on the introduction page ([GRETIL home](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil.html))).
