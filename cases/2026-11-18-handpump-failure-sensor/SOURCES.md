# Sources

Numbered sources for the design study in README.md, cited inline as [S#]. All links are public. Live pages and PDFs were read on 18 November 2026 unless a note says otherwise. Author names are left out on purpose; titles, venues and DOIs identify each work.

## Handpump functionality, repair times and existing sensor projects

- **[S1]** "Functionality of handpump water supplies: a review of data from sub-Saharan Africa and the Asia-Pacific region." *International Journal of Water Resources Development* 36(5):855–869, 2019 (online March 2019). DOI 10.1080/07900627.2018.1543117. Open copy: https://www.waterpointdata.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Functionality-of-handpump-water-supplies.pdf. Read: abstract and results (about one in four handpumps in sub-Saharan Africa non-functional at any time; 175,000 of an estimated 680,000 in 2015 (26%); medians 27–28%; every country above 10%).
- **[S2]** "Risk factors associated with rural water supply failure: A 30-year retrospective study of handpumps on the south coast of Kenya." *Science of the Total Environment* 626:156–164, 2018. DOI 10.1016/j.scitotenv.2017.12.302. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2017.12.302 (an open copy is hosted by the UPGro programme). Read: abstract and methods (571 Afridev sites mapped in Kwale in 2013: 55.0% functional, 41.7% non-functional; failure risk higher with salinity, deeper water level, sand aquifers and distance to spare-part suppliers).
- **[S3]** "GSM-enabled remote monitoring of rural handpumps: a proof-of-concept study." *Journal of Hydroinformatics* 14(4):829–839, 2012. DOI 10.2166/hydro.2012.183. Open copy: https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/115094/1/829.full.pdf. Read: design (accelerometer + microprocessor + GSM modem on the handle), Lusaka trials on India Mk II pumps in July 2011, volume error "under 10%", prototype battery life "of the order of a day", and the statement that power consumption was the biggest remaining technical challenge.
- **[S4]** "From Rights to Results in Rural Water Services – Evidence from Kyuso, Kenya." Smith School of Enterprise and the Environment, University of Oxford, Water Programme Working Paper 1, March 2014. https://www.smithschool.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2022-03/From-Rights-to-Results.pdf. Read: whole report text. Used: 66 Afridev handpumps; mean downtime 27 days to under 3 days; 98% functioning; 89% of repairs within five days; recurring cost USD 62 per repair, USD 8,368 in 2013, about USD 127 per handpump; information costs 27% of recurring costs including one daily message and a replacement battery; battery changes every two years; one daily message with six four-hour blocks would be enough for operations; mobile signal was often absent at handle height in depressions; the report's own phrase "remote surveillance of maintenance service delivery".
- **[S5]** "Evaluating Cellular Instrumentation on Rural Handpumps to Improve Service Delivery—A Longitudinal Study in Rural Rwanda." *Environmental Science & Technology* 49(24):14292–14300, 2015. DOI 10.1021/acs.est.5b04077. Abstract: https://pdxscholar.library.pdx.edu/mengin_fac/80/ ; full text read through the ResearchGate copy and the WaSH Policy Research Digest #8 summary (https://bdd.pseau.org/outils/ouvrages/unc_wash_policy_digest_8_remote_monitoring_handpump_functionality_2018.pdf). Used: 181 sensors in three arms; median time to successful repair about 152 days (nominal), 57 (circuit rider), 21 (sensor-dispatched "ambulance"); functionality about 68%, 73%, 91%; sensors in all arms but data used for dispatch only in the ambulance arm; cost per functional pump per year roughly similar across arms; sensors in Afridev and India Mk II pumps with accelerometer and pressure transducer.
- **[S6]** "Distributed Inference Condition Monitoring System for Rural Infrastructure in the Developing World." *IEEE Sensors Journal* 19(5):1820–1828, 2019. DOI 10.1109/JSEN.2018.2882866. Not read in full; the abstract was seen only as a search-engine summary (96 Hz three-axis accelerometer, on-board novelty filter, cloud models). Figures from it are treated as UNVERIFIED.
- **[S7]** "Handpump maintenance, water use, and health in rural Kenya." Doctoral thesis, University of Reading (redacted copy). https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/105085/ (record page; the redacted PDF is linked from it). Read: abstract (only repairs within 24 hours were associated with lower reported diarrhoea, a 60% reduction; pump use strongly tied to rainfall) and the trial description.
- **[S8]** "Data Improves Water Services: The Smart Handpump Project" case study, SDSN TReNDS, November 2018. https://www.sdsntrends.org/files/s/casestudy_smarthandpump_nov2018.pdf. Read via search-result extract: FundiFix continuation; February–June 2015, 56 repairs to 18 pumps, mean downtime 1.1 days; communities' stated willingness to pay about USD 10 per month (citing a 2018 study not read here).
- **[S9]** University of Oxford news, "RCUK highlights Oxford's 'innovative' smart handpumps project," 2 July 2015. https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2015-07-02-rcuk-highlights-oxfords-innovative-smart-handpumps-project. Used: "up to one third of pumps are out of action at any one time"; fixes "in under two days".

## The other two options

- **[S10]** SoPAS / REPIC, "Retrofit for converting hand pumps into solar pumping systems with tap stations," final report, 11 March 2021 (Benin). https://www.repic.ch/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/FinalReport_SOPAS_2020_fv.pdf. Read: value-proposition section (conventional solarised systems USD 8,000–20,000 with tank; retrofit device about USD 1,000; total solarisation USD 2,000–5,000, both stated as estimates).
- **[S11]** "Effect of in-line drinking water chlorination at the point of collection on child diarrhoea in urban Bangladesh: a double-blind, cluster-randomised controlled trial." *The Lancet Global Health* 7(9):e1247–e1256, 2019. DOI 10.1016/S2214-109X(19)30315-8. https://www.thelancet.com/journals/langlo/article/PIIS2214-109X(19)30315-8/fulltext. Used (from the abstract as summarised in search results): diarrhoea prevalence 10.0% vs 7.5%, prevalence ratio 0.77 (95% CI 0.65–0.91). Setting is shared taps on piped supply, not handpumps.
- **[S12]** Evidence Action, "Safe Water Now" programme page. https://www.evidenceaction.org/programs/safe-water-now. Read: about 10 million people reached, 53K dispensers in active use, cost "less than $1.70 per person, per year", dispensers at rural water points in Kenya, Malawi and Uganda, in-line devices on tanks of piped systems, circuit riders fix issues "within 72 hours". Self-reported figures.

## Financing and data

- **[S13]** Uptime, "Results-Based Contracts for Rural Water Services," working paper, July 2020. https://bdd.pseau.org/outils/ouvrages/uptime_results_based_contracts_for_rural_water_services_2020.pdf. Read: section 3.2 (USD 50 per quarter per waterpoint with quarterly uptime above 96%; USD 1/m³; 1:1 match of local revenue; volume measured "using meters or sensors").
- **[S14]** Water Point Data Exchange (WPdx), Data Submission Policy. https://www.waterpointdata.org/data-submission-policy/. Read: providers license unpublished data to WPdx under CC BY 4.0 and must hold the rights to do so.
- **[S15]** Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019 (No. 24 of 2019), section 25, principles of data protection. Official text: https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2019/24/eng%402022-12-31 ; section text read at https://dataprotection.co.ke/law/section-25 (reproduces the consolidated Kenya Law text). Used: 25(c) purpose limitation, 25(d) data minimisation, 25(g) retention limits, 25(h) transfers outside Kenya.

## Connectivity

- **[S16]** The Star (Kenya), "Kenya among states to phase out 2G and 3G networks by 2030," 25 September 2026. https://www.the-star.co.ke/business/kenya/2026-09-25-kenya-among-states-to-phase-out-2g-and-3g-networks-by-2030. Read via search extract: GSMA roadmap lists 11 African countries (Kenya, Uganda, DRC, Egypt, Ethiopia, Ghana, Niger, Nigeria, Rwanda, Senegal, South Africa) to phase out 2G/3G by 2030; Kenya 2G subscriptions fell 27.3% to 9.3 million by June 2026. The GSMA roadmap itself was not read.
- **[S17]** MyBroadband, "MTN 2G and 3G shutdown plan." https://mybroadband.co.za/news/cellular/602814-mtn-2g-and-3g-shutdown-plan.html ; and "South African mobile networks detail plans to switch off 2G and 3G," https://mybroadband.co.za/news/cellular/523884-south-african-mobile-networks-detail-plans-to-switch-off-2g-and-3g.html. Read via search extract: MTN South Africa plans 3G off end-2026 and 2G off end-2029; an earlier government deadline of 31 December 2027 is no longer enforced.
- **[S18]** 5G Americas, "Release-13 Cellular IoT Deployments – November 2025." https://www.5gamericas.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Cellular-IoT_11-17-2025.pdf. Read: Africa row lists 2 NB-IoT networks (Safaricom Kenya, Vodacom South Africa) and 0 LTE-M.

## Component datasheets

- **[S19]** SIMCom, "A7672X/A7670X Series Hardware Design" V1.03 (third-party mirror; SIMCom's own download was not checked). https://w2.electrodragon.com/Chip-cn-dat/SIMCOM-dat/A7670-DAT/A7672X_A7670X_Series_Hardware_Design_V1.03.pdf. Used: VBAT 3.4–4.2 V (3.8 V recommended); 2 A peak in GSM TX; at least 300 µF bulk capacitance (600 µF if the supply cannot give 2 A) to keep droop under 300 mV; power-off leakage 20 µA typ; sleep/idle currents "TBD"; GSM talk 320 mA (EGSM900) and LTE Cat-1 520–600 mA at 23 dBm (Table 46); 1.8 V UART; band table listing A7672E and A7670E together (GSM 900/1800, LTE-FDD B1/B3/B5/B7/B8/B20).
- **[S20]** SIMCom SIM7080G series datasheet, via the LCSC link for C18548266. Used: Cat-M/NB-IoT, PSM current 3.2 µA, VBAT 2.7–4.8 V.
- **[S21]** STMicroelectronics LIS2DH12 datasheet, via the LCSC link for C110926. Used: Table 12 (low-power mode 2 µA at 1 Hz, 3 µA at 10 Hz, 4 µA at 25 Hz); power-down 0.5 µA; supply 1.71–3.6 V.
- **[S22]** Microchip ATmega48A/PA/88A/PA/168A/PA/328/P datasheet (summary edition), via the LCSC link for C33901. Used: at 1 MHz, 1.8 V, 25 °C: active 0.2 mA, power-down 0.1 µA, power-save 0.75 µA including 32 kHz RTC; 1.8–5.5 V; 0–10 MHz at 2.7–5.5 V.
- **[S23]** Texas Instruments TPS63802 datasheet (Chinese-language edition), via the LCSC link for C2845237. Used: 2 A output at VOUT 3.3 V for VIN ≥ 2.3 V; IQ 11 µA; shutdown current 45 nA typ / 600 nA max; input 1.3–5.5 V; boost peak current limit 4–5.75 A; load disconnect.
- **[S24]** Texas Instruments TPS7A02 datasheet, via the LCSC link for C3747031. Used: 25 nA IQ, 200 mA, fixed 3.0 V version.
- **[S25]** KNSCHA KNE2 470 µF/6.3 V specification (KNE2470UF6.3V149EC0008), via the LCSC link for C2943987. Used: ESR 16 mΩ at 100 kHz; ripple 3.3 A; leakage limit I ≤ 0.2CV or 500 µA (592 µA listed for this part); 2000 h at 105 °C.
- **[S26]** Pulse W3907B0100 "LTE 698–3600 MHz FPC Antenna" datasheet, via the LCSC link for C6750605. Used: band coverage; average total efficiency 55% / 49% at 698–960 MHz (two test conditions).
- **[S27]** Energizer E95 (D, LR20) product datasheet. https://data.energizer.com/pdfs/E95_Max_NA.pdf. Used: nominal IR 150–300 mΩ fresh; operating temperature −18 °C to 55 °C; shelf life 10 years at 21 °C; capacity chart (axis to 24,000 mAh) whose exact values could not be read from the text.
- **[S28]** LCSC product-detail data for every C-number in the BOM and text, read on 18 November 2026 from the JSON that serves the public product pages (human-readable form: `https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C<number>.html`). Fields used: model, maker, package, stock, lifecycle ("productCycle"), minimum buy, price ladder, and parameters (for example inductor Isat and the LDO's IQ). Part searches used the public JLCPCB parts-search API.
