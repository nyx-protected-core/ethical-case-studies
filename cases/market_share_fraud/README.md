# Market share Fraud

Date: 2026-11-19

38 case studies, listed alphabetically.

*The category name is the repository's. It does not label every case as fraud: patent and copyright cases are described as infringement, and "theft" is used only where a court, regulator or plea found theft or misappropriation.*

*Category overview. Research and writing only; not legal advice. Sources were checked on 19 November 2026.*

## What this category covers

Cases where a company was found, by a court, a jury, a regulator or its own plea, to have taken or infringed another firm's technology or rights, or to have used fraud, bribery or collusion, in a contest whose real prize was a market position. The recurring question is whether the penalty, when it finally came, was small next to the market the conduct helped win.

**Inclusion rule.** A company gets its own case only if there is an adjudicated or admitted finding against it: a conviction, a guilty plea, a deferred-prosecution or remediation agreement, a final civil judgment of theft, fraud, misappropriation or infringement, or a regulator's sanction. Filed or pending suits, charges and settlements without findings are listed below under *Alleged, not adjudicated*, not as cases. Patent and copyright infringement are reported as infringement; the word "theft" is used only where a finding of trade-secret theft or misappropriation exists.

**How figures are treated.** Damages are compared with market effect only where a court or official record gives a figure. Government and prosecution figures are flagged for reporting incentives. No case in this category estimates a market share that no record measured.

**Not used here.** This category is global. It uses no Canadian data, institutions or studies. Canadian law and policy appear only in the law sections of the IP-theft-in-engineering review (section 4.2 and the comparison table in section 5.1); no Canadian data, studies or institutions are used there either.

## Repeat offenders

Companies with more than one adjudicated finding in the records opened so far:

- **Alstom.** Before the 2014 US guilty plea, a US subsidiary had resolved corruption charges over a power project in Italy, and Alstom had resolved matters with Switzerland and the World Bank (effective 21 February 2012), as described in the plea agreement. The DOJ cited this prior misconduct in setting the fine. UK subsidiaries were later convicted or pleaded guilty (2018–2019). Record: https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/opa/press-releases/attachments/2014/12/29/alstom_sa_plea_agreement_combined.pdf
- **Apple.** Separate adjudicated findings in different matters: patent infringement found by the ITC (Masimo; affirmed, certiorari petition pending), a final patent-infringement judgment (VirnetX), and an unfair-competition ruling (Epic Games, not intellectual property). The Epic contempt is under Supreme Court review and is not counted as a final finding. Records: https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/24-1285.OPINION.3-19-2026_2663002.pdf ; https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.txed.124653/gov.uscourts.txed.124653.1107.0.pdf ; https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2025/12/11/25-2935.pdf
- **AU Optronics** was convicted in the United States and fined by the European Commission for the same LCD conduct; **Samsung** received immunity in the EU LCD case.
- **Barclays, UBS and RBS** appear in both LIBOR (2012–2013) and forex (2014–2021). Barclays and UBS were found to have breached their 2012 LIBOR non-prosecution agreements through forex-period conduct (the 2015 Justice Department release could not be opened, so the breach amounts are UNVERIFIED).
- **Bridgestone** pleaded guilty twice in the United States: marine hose (2011) and anti-vibration rubber (2014); the second plea notes that it did not disclose the second conspiracy at the time of the first.
- **Citigroup** appears in forex and in the SEC Enron settlement; **Deutsche Bank** has both a LIBOR subsidiary plea and fines in the 2013 interest rate derivatives decision.
- **Ericsson.** A 2019 DPA (FCPA), then a 2023 guilty plea after the DOJ found that Ericsson breached the DPA by failing to disclose evidence. The breach is a disclosure failure, not a new bribery finding. Records: https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/ericsson-agrees-pay-over-1-billion-resolve-fcpa-case ; https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/ericsson-plead-guilty-and-pay-over-206m-following-breach-2019-fcpa-deferred-prosecution
- **Hytera Communications.** A civil judgment (trade secrets and copyright) and a criminal guilty plea, for the same course of conduct, in two separate forums. Records: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca7-22-02370/pdf/USCOURTS-ca7-22-02370-0.pdf ; https://www.justice.gov/usao-ndil/media/1384221/dl?inline=
- **JPMorgan** appears in the forex guilty plea (2015), the spoofing DPA and CFTC order (2020; most of the spoofing predates the 2015 plea) and the SEC Enron settlement (2003).
- **Rimini Street.** Copyright infringement found and affirmed in Oracle's first suit (2018), then civil contempt of the resulting injunction on four issues ($530,000 after the 2023 appeal and remand). Findings in Oracle's second suit were mostly vacated on appeal; a permanent injunction followed in April 2025, and the 2025 settlement is conditional, with dismissal only after a PeopleSoft wind-down due by July 2028; no admission of liability. Records: https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/01/08/16-16832.pdf ; https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/08/24/22-15188.pdf ; https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nvd.71326/gov.uscourts.nvd.71326.1588.0.pdf
- **Samsung Electronics.** Two separate Apple suits each ended in a jury finding of patent infringement that survived appeal: the 2011 suit (2012 and 2018 verdicts) and the 2012 suit ($119,625,000 judgment reinstated en banc in 2016). In the second suit the jury also found that Apple infringed a Samsung patent. Patent infringement does not require intent and is not described here as theft. Records: https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/14-1335.opinion.5-14-2015.1.pdf ; https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/cafc/15-1171/15-1171-2016-10-07.html

- **Volkswagen** has the US guilty plea, the German fine order and the 2021 EU cartel decision; **Daimler** was fined in the trucks cartel and received immunity in the 2021 emissions cartel.
- **Yazaki and Furukawa Electric** were sanctioned for wire harnesses in the United States, the EU and (UNVERIFIED here) Japan.
- **Related, not repeat.** The Rolls-Royce judgment notes a 2012 DOJ DPA with a Rolls-Royce joint venture, a separate entity. Telia and VimpelCom were resolved for bribery in the same Uzbek market, but they are different companies. Odebrecht/Braskem, Glencore, Airbus, Goldman Sachs, Petrobras and Teva each resolved one course of conduct in several jurisdictions at once; parallel resolutions are not counted as repeat findings.

## How to read a case in this category

Each case states the finding and its record, a timeline, the damages or penalties against any measured market effect, an analysis grounded in peer-reviewed work on deterrence and innovation, fair counterarguments (including legitimate intellectual-property protection and the costs of litigation), proposed solutions, what the case does not replace, and the items that remain unverified.

## Note on hardware

Several cases concern physical products (radios, memory chips, wind turbines, fibres, wheels, batteries, phones, a smartwatch sensor, racing cars). None is a hardware design study: no case contains a design, a parts list or build instructions. Each is a legal and ethical analysis of a dispute about such products.
