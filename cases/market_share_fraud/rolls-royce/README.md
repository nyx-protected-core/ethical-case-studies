# Rolls-Royce (2017): UK, U.S. and Brazilian resolutions of bribery to win aero-engine, energy and defence business

Date: 2026-11-19

**Key to flags.** ⚑ marks a figure published by a government, court or intergovernmental body. These are negotiated settlement amounts and agreed estimates of profit, not measures of harm. Prosecutors have reasons to present resolutions as large and successful, and the amounts cover only conduct that was detected and admitted (detection bias).

---

## 1. Summary

On 17 January 2017 the Crown Court, sitting at Southwark, approved a **deferred prosecution agreement (DPA)** between the UK Serious Fraud Office (SFO) and **Rolls-Royce plc** and its U.S. subsidiary **Rolls-Royce Energy Systems Inc. (RRESI)**. The DPA covered bribery, conspiracy to corrupt, false accounting and failure to prevent bribery across **seven countries over about 24 years**. The terms were disgorgement of **£258,170,000 ⚑**, a financial penalty of **£239,082,645 ⚑**, the SFO's costs of **£12,960,754 ⚑**, and a completed compliance programme. The total exceeded **£½ billion ⚑** [R1].

At the same time, Rolls-Royce entered a DPA with the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) on a one-count FCPA conspiracy information, with a penalty of **$169,917,710 ⚑** [R3]. It also reached a leniency settlement with Brazil's Ministério Público Federal of **$25,579,179 ⚑** [R3]. The U.S. DPA ended with the charge dismissed **with prejudice** in May 2020 [R4], [R5].

**Evidence strength: strong.** The approved judgment, the U.S. DPA and the dismissal order were all opened. The facts were agreed by the company in Statements of Facts.

## 4. How the scheme worked, as found in the record

- **Agents and intermediaries.** Most counts involve payments to agents or intermediaries, some passed on to officials, in connection with engine sales, energy equipment and defence work [R1, ¶4]. The U.S. Statement of Facts describes one intermediary hired as a commercial agent "on at least 7 projects" in Angola, Azerbaijan, Iraq, Kazakhstan and elsewhere [R3].
- **State-owned customers.** The U.S. record names state-controlled buyers, including Petrobras (Brazil), the State Oil Company of the Azerbaijan Republic, a Kazakh–Chinese gas pipeline company, and a Thai state-linked energy group [R3].
- **Concealment.** In India the use of intermediaries was hidden while it was restricted, and a corrupt payment was made to recover a list of intermediaries seized by a tax inspector [R1, ¶4(ii)–(iii)].
- **Scale.** The court recorded that the conduct "ultimately earned over £250 million of gross profit" on the agreed figures. This is an estimate agreed between the parties [R1].

## 5. Monitorships and aftermath

- **No formal monitor; a self-appointed "quasi-monitor".** The court relied on the company's 2013 appointment of an external reviewer and on a "compliance programme" funded by the company. The court recorded that programme at about **£15 million** [R1, ¶¶43, 68].
- **Cost of cooperation.** Rolls-Royce's investigation and cooperation costs were **£123,115,643** as at December 2016 [R1, ¶39].
- **Related matters cited by the court.** A 2012 DOJ DPA with a Rolls-Royce joint venture over civil nuclear bribery (1999–2004), and a 2016 settlement by German prosecutors with a company that Rolls-Royce later acquired. The court treated neither as relevant to Rolls-Royce's own position [R1, ¶¶40–41].
- **The procuring country.** An Indonesian peer-reviewed article analyses the Garuda Indonesia–Rolls-Royce bribery as transnational crime and discusses Indonesian enforcement [P1]. Only the abstract was read. **Open question:** the results of Indonesian court proceedings were not verified from primary records and are not stated here.

## 7. Fair counterarguments

1. **Do DPAs deter, or do they exempt?** Lord (2022) analyses how judges in England and Wales justify approving DPAs. He identifies three mechanisms ("deviance elastication; corporate dissociation; and anticipatory offsetting") and argues the result is an infrastructure in which corporations are "exempt, not just deferred, from criminal prosecution" [P2]. The court in this case gave the opposite view. A conviction could trigger debarment under public procurement rules: about 15% of the order book at the end of 2014 came from buyers in countries with mandatory debarment, and about another 15% from countries with discretionary debarment. Debarment would harm third parties, including the UK defence industry, the supply chain and competition in concentrated markets. The court also held that cooperation of this scale deserved the DPA route [R1, ¶¶52–56].
2. **Not a self-report, yet treated like one.** The court accepted that the investigation "was not triggered by a self-report", because the SFO had been alerted by internet postings. It nonetheless agreed not to distinguish Rolls-Royce's "extraordinary" cooperation from a self-report [R1, ¶¶21–22]. Critics read this as weakening the incentive to report first. Supporters say it rewards full disclosure once the conduct is known.
3. **Company resolved; no individual charged in the UK.** The DPA covered conduct that, in the court's words, "implicated senior management and, on the face of it, controlling minds of the company" [R1, ¶4]. Yet no individual was charged in the UK [R7]. In the US, the DOJ brought related cases against individuals [R6]. Arlen (2016) argues that pre-trial diversion agreements give prosecutors broad discretion without sufficient oversight [P3]. The individual result is the clearest test of that concern here.
4. **Profit kept versus profit disgorged.** The disgorgement of £258m sits beside "over £250 million" of gross profit on the agreed figures [R1]. Cheung, Rau & Stouraitis (2021) find that the net present value of a bribe conditional on being caught is close to zero for the median firm [P4]. Whether this resolution removed all the gain depends on profits from conduct that was never charged, which is unknown.
5. **Multi-country crediting.** The U.S. credited the Brazilian payment and the UK did not cover U.S. conduct [R1], [R3]. Bu (2022) argues for a predictable global settlement regime [P5].

**How this case handles them.** The case keeps its conclusion that Rolls-Royce admitted bribery and failures to prevent bribery across seven countries, because that rests on an approved UK judgment and a US DPA with a statement of facts. It reports the court's own reasons for the DPA, including the harm a conviction could do to third parties through debarment, alongside Lord's critique, and leaves the choice between them to the reader. The no-charge decision is limited to the UK and rests on the SFO's own statement.

## 9. Values and proposals (normative)

1. **Tie DPA credit to *who* found the conduct.** Keep full self-report credit for companies that come forward before any outside alert. Where an investigation was triggered externally, as here [R1, ¶¶21–22], cap the discount. *Basis:* Lord's critique of anticipatory offsetting [P2]. *Evidence rating: untested (UNTESTED). Lord (2022) is a critique, not an evaluation.*
2. **Require a public decision record when individuals are not charged after a corporate DPA.** The record should give reasons, within legal limits. *Basis:* the gap between the "controlling minds" finding [R1, ¶4] and the absence of UK charges [R7]; Arlen on oversight of prosecutorial discretion [P3]. *Evidence rating: untested (UNTESTED). No evaluation of published non-charging reasons was found.*
3. **Use independent monitors, not company-appointed reviewers, when conduct spans decades and countries.** *Basis:* the reliance on a company-appointed "quasi-monitor" [R1, ¶43]; Arlen [P3]. *Evidence rating: untested (UNTESTED). Arlen (2016) is a legal argument, not an evaluation.*
4. **Coordinate restitution to procuring states.** Indonesia, Thailand, Nigeria and others were the places harmed [R1], but the money went to UK, U.S. and Brazilian treasuries. A share should go to accountable programmes in the affected states. *Basis:* [R1], [R3]; Prakasa on the Indonesian perspective [P1]. *Evidence rating: untested (UNTESTED). No evaluation of restitution to procuring states was found.*
5. **Disgorge on a full-profit basis.** Where profits cannot be fully measured, the presumption should favour the higher estimate. *Basis:* Cheung et al. on returns to bribery [P4]. *Evidence rating: untested (UNTESTED). Cheung et al. (2021) is correlational and does not test disgorgement rules.*

## 10. Recommendation (normative)

Use Rolls-Royce as the reference case for the **DPA debate**. It shows a very large corporate resolution with extensive cooperation and no individual prosecutions. Readers should weigh the court's reasoning [R1] against Lord's critique [P2] and form their own view.

## 12. What this does not replace

This case is not a finding against any individual; none was charged in the UK on the information available. It does not replace the approved judgment, the DPA or the statement of facts. It is not a current compliance assessment of Rolls-Royce or legal advice on UK DPAs or the FCPA.
