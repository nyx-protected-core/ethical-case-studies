# Sources

All sources were checked on 18 November 2026.

**How sources were verified**

- **DOIs:** resolved through doi.org (HTTP 302 to the publisher's page) *and* metadata (title,
  authors, journal, year) confirmed through the Crossref API. Abstracts were read on the publisher's
  page or through the Semantic Scholar API.
- **URLs:** fetched directly. For reports, the PDF was downloaded and the quoted figures were found
  in the text.
- **Status values:** VERIFIED (exists and supports the stated claim), PARTLY VERIFIED (exists, but
  the content could not be fully read), UNVERIFIED (could not be confirmed; not relied on).

## Peer-reviewed scholarship

**[A1]** Musto, J. L. and boyd, d. (2014). The Trafficking-Technology Nexus. *Social Politics:
International Studies in Gender, State & Society*, 21(3), 461–483.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/sp/jxu018
Supports: anti-trafficking technology has expanded surveillance and a law-and-order agenda;
caution about tech optimism.
Status: VERIFIED (DOI resolves; Crossref metadata matches; abstract read on publisher page).

**[A2]** Milivojevic, S., Moore, H. and Segrave, M. (2020). Freeing the Modern Slaves, One Click at
a Time: Theorising human trafficking, modern slavery, and technology. *Anti-Trafficking Review*,
14, 16–32.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.14197/atr.201220142
Supports: technology framed as both problem and solution; need for empirical evidence; technology
should not distract from root causes.
Status: VERIFIED (DOI resolves; Crossref metadata and abstract confirmed).

**[A3]** Musto, J., Thakor, M. and Gerasimov, B. (2020). Editorial: Between Hope and Hype: Critical
evaluations of technology's role in anti-trafficking. *Anti-Trafficking Review*, 14, 1–14.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.14197/atr.201220141
Supports: critique of "tech solutionist fixes"; trafficking needs political will.
Status: VERIFIED (DOI resolves; abstract read on journal page).

**[A4]** Zimmerman, C. and Kiss, L. (2017). Human trafficking and exploitation: A global health
concern. *PLOS Medicine*, 14(11), e1002437.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1002437
Supports: labour migration benefits millions; exploitation sustained by "usurious labor
intermediaries"; prevention across the migration cycle.
Status: VERIFIED (DOI resolves; summary points read on publisher page).

**[A5]** Thinyane, H. and Bhat, K. S. (2019). Apprise: Supporting the Critical-Agency of Victims of
Human Trafficking in Thailand. *Proceedings of the 2019 CHI Conference on Human Factors in
Computing Systems*, 1–14.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300385
Supports: existence of Apprise; co-design with over 170 stakeholders using Value Sensitive Design.
Status: VERIFIED (DOI resolves; Crossref metadata and abstract confirmed).
Note: a similar DOI ending in 3300355 was checked first and turned out to be an unrelated CHI
paper; it was not used.

**[A6]** Sassetti, F. and Thinyane, H. (2021). Apprise: inclusive innovation for enhancing the agency
of vulnerable populations in the context of anti-trafficking responses. *Innovation and
Development*, 13(1), 173–191.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/2157930X.2020.1854249
Supports: users will "(mis)use" technology for their own purposes; privacy and welfare risks for
vulnerable workers using digital tools.
Status: VERIFIED (DOI resolves; Crossref metadata and abstract confirmed).

**[A7]** Franceschini, I., Li, L., Hu, Y. and Bo, M. (2024). A new type of victim? Profiling
survivors of modern slavery in the online scam industry in Southeast Asia. *Trends in Organized
Crime*. Published online 6 November 2024.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s12117-024-09552-2
Supports: many scam-compound victims were deceived by fraudulent job ads, others by friends or
relatives, some kidnapped; call for better information-sharing on recruitment.
Status: VERIFIED (DOI resolves; abstract read on publisher page).

**[A8]** Dottridge, M. (2017). Global Trafficking Prevalence Data Distorts Efforts to Stop Patterns of
Human Trafficking. *Anti-Trafficking Review*, 8.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.14197/atr.201217812
Supports: caution about relying on global prevalence figures.
Status: VERIFIED (DOI resolves; article page and title confirmed; only the short abstract was read,
so it is cited for its stated argument only).

## UN, ILO and IOM reports and pages

**[S1]** ILO, Walk Free and IOM (2022). *Global Estimates of Modern Slavery: Forced Labour and Forced
Marriage* (2021 estimates). Report page:
https://www.ilo.org/publications/major-publications/global-estimates-modern-slavery-forced-labour-and-forced-marriage
Executive summary PDF:
https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/@ed_norm/@ipec/documents/publication/wcms_854733.pdf
Supports: 49.6 million in modern slavery; 27.6 million in forced labour; 22 million in forced
marriage; more than 3.3 million children in forced labour; adult migrant workers more than three
times as likely to be in forced labour.
Status: VERIFIED (figures found in executive summary PDF text).

**[S2]** ILO (2024). *Profits and Poverty: The economics of forced labour*.
https://www.ilo.org/publications/major-publications/profits-and-poverty-economics-forced-labour
PDF: https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2024-10/Profits%20and%20poverty%20-%20The%20economics%20of%20forced%20labour_WEB_20241017.pdf
Supports: US$236 billion annual illegal profits; close to US$10,000 per victim; US$5.6 billion and
15 per cent of illegal profits from international migrants in forced labour come from recruitment
fees and costs (26 per cent in forced labour exploitation); average 3.93 months of work to repay
recruitment costs (KNOMAD data); fees can lead to debt bondage.
Status: VERIFIED (figures found in PDF text).

**[S3]** ILO (2019). *General principles and operational guidelines for fair recruitment and
Definition of recruitment fees and related costs*.
https://www.ilo.org/publications/general-principles-and-operational-guidelines-fair-recruitment-and
Supports: workers should not be charged, directly or indirectly, any recruitment fees or related
costs.
Status: VERIFIED (page and PDF fetched; wording found).

**[S4]** UNODC (2024). *Global Report on Trafficking in Persons 2024*.
Press release (11 December 2024):
https://www.unodc.org/unodc/en/press/releases/2024/December/unodc-global-human-trafficking-report_-detected-victims-up-25-per-cent-as-more-children-are-exploited-and-forced-labour-cases-spike.html
Special points of interest: https://www.unodc.org/documents/data-and-analysis/glotip/2024/GLOTIP24_special_points_final.pdf
Supports: detected victims up 25 per cent (2022 vs 2019); forced labour detections up 47 per cent;
42 per cent forced labour vs 36 per cent sexual exploitation in 2022; forced criminality from 1 per
cent (2016) to 8 per cent (2022); 156 countries covered.
Status: VERIFIED (press release and special points PDF fetched).
Note: UNODC lists a 2026 edition as scheduled for November 2026. It was not confirmed as
published at the time of research, so the 2024 edition is used. Check for the newer edition
before publishing.

**[S5]** OHCHR (2023). *Online Scam Operations and Trafficking into Forced Criminality in Southeast
Asia: Recommendations for a Human Rights Response*. Briefing paper, August 2023.
https://bangkok.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/wp%5Ffiles/2023/08/ONLINE-SCAM-OPERATIONS-2582023.pdf
Supports: at least 120,000 (Myanmar) and at least 100,000 (Cambodia) may be held in forced scam
work; recruitment via ads for professional roles on major social media platforms; passport
confiscation and debt bondage; recommendation that platforms address fraudulent job ads; the
difficulty of identifying fake ads "without restricting people's rights".
Status: VERIFIED (PDF downloaded; passages found in text). The OHCHR press release page for the
same report blocked automated fetching and is not cited.

**[S6]** ILO. Fair recruitment topic page (Fair Recruitment Initiative).
https://www.ilo.org/topics-and-sectors/fair-recruitment
Supports: the Fair Recruitment Initiative exists and was launched in 2014.
Status: VERIFIED.

**[S7]** IOM. *IRIS: Ethical Recruitment*. https://iris.iom.int/
Supports: IRIS promotes ethical recruitment, the Employer Pays Principle, voluntary certification of
recruitment agencies and stronger regulation.
Status: VERIFIED (page read; direct command-line fetch was refused, the page fetch tool succeeded).
